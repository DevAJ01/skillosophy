# Rawlsian Institutional Fairness

Examine whether institutional rules can be justified to people occupying different positions. This adapts Rawls's justice as fairness; a small organizational allocation problem is an analogy, not automatically the basic structure of society.

## Method

Identify the rule system, goods or burdens allocated, and the people affected. Compare feasible alternatives, including the existing rules. Distinguish formal access from effective opportunity; time, resources, disability, and institutional position can alter the ability to benefit.

Consider the choice of principles without knowledge of one's particular social position, talents, or conception of a good life. The veil of ignorance changes the information available for choosing principles; it is not merely an invitation to feel sympathy, and it does not by itself justify every risk-averse choice.

For a justice-as-fairness analysis, examine equal basic liberties first, fair equality of opportunity next, and inequalities under the difference principle after those. Do not trade away basic liberties for a larger aggregate payoff. Do not identify the difference principle with equalizing every outcome or simply maximizing the smallest number in an arbitrary table.

Specify who counts as least advantaged for the institutional context and why the measure is relevant. Distinguish resource position from personal preference, and avoid unsupported claims about which groups benefit. Ask whether permitted inequalities improve that position relative to feasible alternatives. Flag missing evidence rather than assuming a trickle-down benefit.

Propose a rule with accessible entry conditions, safeguards against capture, and a review of distributional effects. Explain limitations of transferring the framework beyond its original scope.

## Useful output

Compare rules across liberties, effective opportunity, and the position of the least advantaged. Recommend a rule and state the evidence that would challenge its fairness.

## Worked application — original example

Request: “Allocate training places by first-come-first-served registration at noon.”
A formally equal registration window may favor people with flexible schedules and immediate access. Compare a longer application window and selection among eligible applicants, with support for people facing access barriers. Evaluate the actual distribution rather than assuming an open link creates fair opportunity.

## Failure checks

Have you collapsed fairness into total benefit or identical shares? Have you skipped liberties and opportunity? Have you defined the least advantaged opportunistically to support your recommendation?
