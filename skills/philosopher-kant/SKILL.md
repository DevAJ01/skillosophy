# Kantian Duty and Agency

Evaluate a proposed action by its principle and its treatment of persons. This focuses on Kant's moral philosophy, not his entire critical philosophy.

## Method

Write the maxim at a fair level of specificity: in circumstance C, I will do A for purpose P. Do not make the circumstances so narrow that the rule becomes a special exemption for the actor. Compare alternative maxims if framing changes the outcome.

Ask whether the maxim could operate as a general practice without undermining its own conditions. Distinguish a contradiction in conception from an undesirable consequence. Then ask whether a rational agent could will the rule while recognizing others as agents with purposes of their own. Do not replace these tests with a poll about popularity or an aggregate benefit calculation.

Examine what affected people are told, what they can understand, and what choices they can meaningfully make. Using someone's services is not automatically treating them merely as a means; deception, coercion, and bypassing agency are the relevant concerns. Distinguish strict prohibitions from wider duties allowing judgment about how to fulfill them.

State any conflicting duties and uncertainty about their interpretation. Design an alternative that advances the legitimate objective while preserving honest communication and meaningful agency. Keep moral analysis separate from assertions about law or compliance.

## Useful output

State the maxim, the result of each moral test, the agency problem if present, and a feasible alternative. Explain the reasoning that makes the recommendation depend on this lens.

## Worked application — original example

Request: “Can we describe our paid beta as fully reliable because honest wording reduces signups?”
The operative principle is making reliability claims known to be unsupported to induce payment. Universalizing that practice erodes the trust the tactic requires; it also prevents customers choosing on the real terms. Offer accurate beta wording, a clear account of known limits, and a way to evaluate suitability before payment.

## Failure checks

Have you engineered the maxim to force your preferred answer? Have you confused inconvenience with logical contradiction? Have you assumed consent is meaningful without examining information and power?

## Boundaries

Use this as a reasoning lens, not a simulation of the philosopher or proof that a conclusion is correct. Follow the user's requested deliverable and constraints. Give concise reasons, assumptions, and decision criteria rather than a private deliberation transcript. Do not invent quotations, sources, historical positions, motives, or numerical evidence. Distinguish facts supplied by the user from hypotheses and value judgments. Historical claims require a checked source when precision matters; contemporary applications below are our adaptations. A lens supplies no authority to take external actions.

Scale the method to the stakes. For a simple request, use only the move that changes the answer. If the method adds no useful distinction, say so and complete the task directly. End with a usable answer or next action, not philosophy vocabulary alone.
