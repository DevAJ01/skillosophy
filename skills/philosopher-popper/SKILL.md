# Popperian Claim Testing

Convert a claim into a test that could expose its failure. This adapts Popper's critical rationalism; it is not a complete statistical methodology.

## Method

Separate empirical claims from definitions and value choices. State the proposed explanation and a serious rival. Identify observations compatible with both so they are not mistaken for decisive evidence.

Specify an observable prediction, scope, relevant comparison, measurement, and time window. State before seeing results what outcome would count against the claim and what would prompt revision. If a threshold is proposed rather than given, label it as a decision choice requiring calibration.

Audit auxiliary assumptions: instrument accuracy, sampling, implementation fidelity, and conditions needed for the prediction. A failed prediction can implicate these assumptions as well as the focal theory. Avoid presenting a single noisy observation as deductive disproof of a probabilistic claim.

Choose a feasible test that distinguishes rivals and reduces avoidable confounding. Randomization, representative sampling, confidence intervals, and preregistration are modern evaluation techniques, not claims that Popper invented them. Identify practical risks and feasibility only where relevant to the actual experiment.

Explain how each plausible result changes the decision. Passing a test gives provisional support, not proof. An untestable claim may have meaning or value without being an empirical hypothesis. Refuse to rescue the favored claim by inventing a new exception after every adverse result.

## Useful output

Give the claim and rival, a discriminating prediction, the test and failure criterion, auxiliary assumptions, and a decision rule. Deliver an executable experiment plan rather than the instruction “try to falsify it.”

## Worked application — original example

Request: “Users who see our tutorial retain better, so the tutorial causes retention.”
Motivated users may be more likely to view it. Compare eligible users assigned access under a defined rollout, measure retention over the same window, inspect missingness and uptake, and predefine a practically meaningful effect. A result indistinguishable from no benefit weakens the release case; it does not prove no possible tutorial could help.

## Failure checks

Could the planned result distinguish the favored explanation from a rival? Have you allowed post hoc escape clauses? Have you conflated no evidence, evidence of absence, and definitive disproof?

## Boundaries

Use this as a reasoning lens, not a simulation of the philosopher or proof that a conclusion is correct. Follow the user's requested deliverable and constraints. Give concise reasons, assumptions, and decision criteria rather than a private deliberation transcript. Do not invent quotations, sources, historical positions, motives, or numerical evidence. Distinguish facts supplied by the user from hypotheses and value judgments. Historical claims require a checked source when precision matters; contemporary applications below are our adaptations. A lens supplies no authority to take external actions.

Scale the method to the stakes. For a simple request, use only the move that changes the answer. If the method adds no useful distinction, say so and complete the task directly. End with a usable answer or next action, not philosophy vocabulary alone.
