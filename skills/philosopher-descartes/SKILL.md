# Cartesian Assumption Audit

Separate what is supported from what is merely inherited, then rebuild a practical argument. This adapts Descartes's methodological doubt and decomposition without imposing his metaphysical conclusions on modern tasks.

## Method

State the conclusion and identify its load-bearing premises. Classify direct observations, reported observations, inference, conventions, and uncertain assumptions. A direct observation can still depend on an instrument or interpretation; distinguish observation from infallibility.

Ask what realistic source of error could defeat each premise: mistaken identity, unreliable measurement, missing cases, an ambiguous inference, or an unstated dependency. Radical doubt can clarify foundations, but ordinary decisions do not require indubitable knowledge. Do not treat anything merely conceivable as equally plausible.

Break a compound claim into smaller claims that can be examined independently. Track dependencies so uncertainty in one component does not automatically invalidate unrelated components. Rebuild from the best-supported premises and mark where a defeasible inference enters.

Choose the cheapest check that could resolve the uncertainty most consequential to the decision. State the stopping condition: enough evidence to support the action at the stated stakes, with residual uncertainty visible. Do not promise certainty through introspection or substitute confidence for evidence.

Deliver the revised conclusion or a bounded next step. If evidence cannot establish the original conclusion, give the narrower conclusion it actually supports.

## Useful output

Give an assumption ledger, a dependency that matters, the highest-value verification, and a reconstructed recommendation with uncertainty.

## Worked application — original example

Request: “Every customer wants feature X because three interviewees mentioned it.”
The observations concern three interviewees, while the conclusion concerns all customers. Check recruitment and customer segments; distinguish expressed interest from actual use. Rebuild the claim as a hypothesis about the sampled group, then choose a broader test before committing a full development cycle.

## Failure checks

Have you demanded impossible certainty? Have you doubted everything without prioritization? Does the revised argument make its evidence and remaining dependence visible?

## Boundaries

Use this as a reasoning lens, not a simulation of the philosopher or proof that a conclusion is correct. Follow the user's requested deliverable and constraints. Give concise reasons, assumptions, and decision criteria rather than a private deliberation transcript. Do not invent quotations, sources, historical positions, motives, or numerical evidence. Distinguish facts supplied by the user from hypotheses and value judgments. Historical claims require a checked source when precision matters; contemporary applications below are our adaptations. A lens supplies no authority to take external actions.

Scale the method to the stakes. For a simple request, use only the move that changes the answer. If the method adds no useful distinction, say so and complete the task directly. End with a usable answer or next action, not philosophy vocabulary alone.
