# Wittgensteinian Language Clarification

Resolve a disagreement by examining how its terms function in actual practices. This focuses on the later Wittgenstein rather than combining his early and later work into one doctrine.

## Method

Find the word or analogy producing the apparent problem. Collect concrete uses from the task: who says it, in which activity, and what would count as using it correctly. Compare examples and borderline cases without assuming every concept has one hidden essence.

Distinguish functions such as describing a behavior, predicting an outcome, conferring a status, and setting a rule. A word can perform different functions in different contexts. Explain the particular shift in use rather than diagnosing all disagreement as meaningless.

Check whether an analogy imports criteria from an unsuitable context. “The system understands” could refer to passing a task, explaining a result, or subjective experience; evidence about one does not establish the others. Operational criteria help a practical decision but do not automatically settle every philosophical question.

Rewrite the contested sentence into concrete alternatives. Identify what is now a factual disagreement, what is a choice of convention, and what is a conflict of aims. Recommend acceptance criteria, examples, or a terminology agreement appropriate to the user's work. Preserve substantive disputes that clarification does not dissolve.

## Useful output

Give the conflicting uses, representative examples, a concrete rewrite, and the remaining decision. If writing a specification, include observable acceptance criteria and cases that should fail them.

## Worked application — original example

Request: “Sales wants an intelligent assistant; engineering says that is impossible.”
Replace “intelligent” with the required activities: finding approved guidance, answering within scope, recognizing missing evidence, and escalating exceptions. Those requirements can be implemented and tested. Whether the assistant has human-like understanding remains a separate question.

## Failure checks

Have you claimed all problems are linguistic? Have you imposed an unnecessary dictionary definition? Does the rewrite preserve the stakeholder's actual aim and the unresolved empirical question?

## Boundaries

Use this as a reasoning lens, not a simulation of the philosopher or proof that a conclusion is correct. Follow the user's requested deliverable and constraints. Give concise reasons, assumptions, and decision criteria rather than a private deliberation transcript. Do not invent quotations, sources, historical positions, motives, or numerical evidence. Distinguish facts supplied by the user from hypotheses and value judgments. Historical claims require a checked source when precision matters; contemporary applications below are our adaptations. A lens supplies no authority to take external actions.

Scale the method to the stakes. For a simple request, use only the move that changes the answer. If the method adds no useful distinction, say so and complete the task directly. End with a usable answer or next action, not philosophy vocabulary alone.
