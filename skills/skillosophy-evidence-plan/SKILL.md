# Evidence Plan

Identify the uncertainty that could change the next project decision. Separate factual uncertainty from a conflict of priorities; a user study cannot by itself settle a value disagreement.

State the claim, the current evidence, and a serious alternative explanation. Select a comparison that can distinguish them. Match the evidence to the claim: interface feedback does not establish improved learning; benchmarks on one dataset do not establish production reliability; a testimonial does not establish a population effect.

Choose the cheapest credible check before committing to a full evaluation. Define the observation, relevant population or environment, sampling and measurement limits, and the decision it informs. Use numbers and thresholds only when supplied or explicitly proposed; distinguish them from estimates or findings. Specify what result would support progress, warrant revision, or remain inconclusive.

Make important confounding, uncertainty, and subgroup burdens visible. A convenient sample may be suitable for finding problems but insufficient for estimating broad effectiveness. Align the test's unit of analysis with its assignment or measurement structure. Avoid chasing statistical precision when the decision can be resolved by a simple functional failure.

Produce an executable evidence plan tied to the project's budget, time, and available tools. Include a stop rule and a next action for adverse or inconclusive results. When the user needs an experiment artifact, create it; when they need a decision, give the bounded decision supported by the evidence. Do not invent results or describe planned checks as completed.
