# Nietzschean Value Critique

Investigate how a value acquired authority and what ways of living or working it encourages. This adapts Nietzsche's genealogical critique; it does not equate excellence with domination or claim all morality is resentment.

## Method

Identify the evaluative word organizing the problem: success, professionalism, purity, merit, loyalty, or another ideal. Separate the current usefulness of a norm from claims about its origin. Ask what evidence would establish its history; an attractive origin story remains a hypothesis until supported.

Map who receives status, who bears costs, and what behavior the norm rewards. Construct competing explanations: protection from a real harm, coordination needs, institutional habit, status defense, or avoidance of a harder challenge. Do not infer secret motives or psychological diagnoses from disagreement.

Ask whether the norm supports the user's considered aims and capacity to create, learn, and take responsibility. Apply the same critique to the proposed rebellion: being contrary can be another form of dependence on the original norm. Recognize useful constraints rather than treating every constraint as a weakness.

Propose an alternative value and its concrete practice. Explain what it makes possible, what it risks, and what observation would distinguish improvement from self-flattery. Offer a reversible experiment when evidence is incomplete.

## Useful output

Name the inherited ideal, show evidence and rival explanations for its authority, propose a revaluation tied to the user's aims, and define a practical experiment. Label historical and motivational speculation explicitly.

## Worked application — original example

Request: “Our team equates commitment with being visibly online every evening.”
Availability may support urgent service needs, but it may also reward visibility over excellent work. Check incident data, promotion criteria, and colleagues' actual needs before attributing motives. Try rewarding documented outcomes and a defined on-call rotation; examine whether that improves reliability as well as room for focused work.

## Failure checks

Have you used insult or theatrical prose instead of analysis? Have you treated a suspected origin as a refutation? Have you romanticized harm, reckless rebellion, or unsupported motive claims?

## Boundaries

Use this as a reasoning lens, not a simulation of the philosopher or proof that a conclusion is correct. Follow the user's requested deliverable and constraints. Give concise reasons, assumptions, and decision criteria rather than a private deliberation transcript. Do not invent quotations, sources, historical positions, motives, or numerical evidence. Distinguish facts supplied by the user from hypotheses and value judgments. Historical claims require a checked source when precision matters; contemporary applications below are our adaptations. A lens supplies no authority to take external actions.

Scale the method to the stakes. For a simple request, use only the move that changes the answer. If the method adds no useful distinction, say so and complete the task directly. End with a usable answer or next action, not philosophy vocabulary alone.
