# Philosophical Council

Use complementary methods to reveal a decision's tradeoffs. A council is warranted when one perspective misses a material concern; it is not a majority-vote truth machine.

## Choose and frame

State the same decision, alternatives, facts, unknowns, user constraints, and requested deliverable for every lens. Choose two or three from [the selector catalog](../philosopher-selector/references/catalog.md), and load each chosen SKILL.md. Typical useful contrasts are Kant with Mill for agency versus aggregate effects, Rawls with Mill for institutional fairness versus total benefit, and Popper with Nietzsche for testable explanations versus inherited values. These are suggestions, not mandatory pairs.

## Separate analyses

Apply each lens to the shared facts before synthesis. Keep each analysis focused on its method's strongest relevant contribution, a practical recommendation, and what would change it. Do not let one lens invent facts to defeat another. Independent agents can reduce cross-contamination when the user or host authorizes delegation; provide each with the same facts and its skill, without other lenses' answers. Otherwise perform sequential analyses and explicitly state they are perspectives from one model, not independent deliberators.

## Locate disagreement

Distinguish disagreement about facts, inference, meaning, values, or priority. Correct factual contradictions with evidence rather than counting votes. If one lens's concern is absent from another lens's score, make the omission visible. Do not imply all philosophical theories agree simply because they endorse one practical option for different reasons.

## Synthesize for action

Compare feasible alternatives against the distinct concerns. Look for an option that resolves a conflict without obscuring the sacrifice it requires. If no option satisfies every criterion, state the tradeoff and whose burden remains. Explain which user value or constraint governs the recommendation. Do not average incommensurable judgments into a pseudo-objective score or claim a consensus you do not have.

Produce the requested decision, policy, or artifact, along with the most consequential uncertainty and a review or reversal condition. The synthesis can prefer one lens for a stated reason; unresolved value conflicts can remain visible. If the task is too simple to benefit, complete it without manufacturing a debate.

## Useful output

Use a compact comparison when it helps: lens, decisive concern, recommendation, and revision condition. Follow with the recommended action, the reason for choosing it, and the unresolved tradeoff. Attribute all contemporary reasoning to the lens application, not to historical quotations or endorsements.
