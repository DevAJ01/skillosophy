# Independent philosopher-skill quality review

Reviewed the ten philosopher `SKILL.md` files and their ten `references/sources.md` files under `/Users/ashanj/Documents/ChatGPT/Philosopher Skills/skills/`. Excluded selector, council, evaluation cases/results, project conversation, and other agents' outputs. No repository files were edited. This is a forward review of instruction quality and philosophical fidelity, not an effectiveness score.

## Result

No clear material historical misattribution, fabricated source, or systematic harmful method caricature was found. The instructions explicitly describe contemporary adaptations and consistently separate their operational applications from historical attribution. One low-severity ambiguity merits a narrow correction.

### Low severity: make the Rawlsian difference principle's comparative requirement explicit

- **Location:** `skills/philosopher-rawls/SKILL.md`, lines 16–18, especially line 18: “Ask whether permitted inequalities improve that position relative to feasible alternatives.”
- **Issue:** “Improve” can be read as accepting any arrangement that helps the least advantaged compared with the status quo. The difference principle instead requires their greatest benefit among feasible institutional arrangements satisfying the prior principles. Line 12 already requires comparing alternatives, so this is an ambiguity rather than an unequivocal false statement. The line 16 warning against maximizing an arbitrary table is appropriate, but it leaves the positive comparative criterion unstated.
- **Practical consequence:** A model could accept an unequal policy because it improves the position of the least advantaged, although another feasible policy, equally compliant with liberties and fair opportunity, improves it more. This would weaken the lens's distinct decision criterion.
- **Narrow correction:** Replace the quoted sentence with: “Among feasible arrangements satisfying the prior principles, ask which gives the greatest benefit to the least advantaged, using the justified measure for this context; an improvement over the status quo alone is insufficient.” Preserve the scope warning about organizational analogy and the warning about arbitrary numerical tables.
- **Evidence:** [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, John Rawls, sections 4.3 and 4.9](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rawls/) explains the greatest-benefit requirement and its priority relations. This is **secondary scholarship**, including its citation to *Justice as Fairness: A Restatement*, pp. 42–43; the underlying primary passage was not independently checked in its published edition here.

## What was checked and why the other apparent simplifications were not findings

- **Aristotle:** Context-relative mean, habituation, attention to particulars, and exclusion of intrinsically wrong acts from moderation align with the linked *Nicomachean Ethics* Books II and VI. The skills avoid transferring ancient social hierarchy or attributing a natural end to a modern institution without argument. Primary translated texts: [Book II](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html), [Book VI](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.6.vi.html).
- **Descartes:** Doubt and decomposition are correctly located in *Discourse on Method* Parts IV and II. Defeasible verification and practical stopping rules are explicitly adaptations, so their departure from indubitable foundations is not misattribution. The linked Gutenberg work and primary-text page were checked: [Discourse on Method](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59/pg59-images.html).
- **Epictetus:** The core distinction concerning agency and judgments is grounded in *Enchiridion* 1 and 5. The influence category, humane emotional framing, and contemporary accountability recommendations are presented as adaptations rather than verbatim ancient doctrine. Primary translated text: [Enchiridion](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html).
- **Kant:** The maxim, universalization, and agency tests are appropriate focused adaptations. The text distinguishes mere use from using a person merely as a means and cautions against substituting aggregate consequence calculations. It does not claim to exhaust Kant's moral philosophy. The source link identifies the correct primary translated work: [Groundwork / Fundamental Principles](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5682/pg5682-images.html).
- **Mill:** Qualitative welfare, justice, security, and general rules are appropriate themes; modern expected-value machinery is expressly disclaimed as historical attribution. The source link identifies the correct primary work: [Utilitarianism](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11224/pg11224-images.html).
- **Nietzsche:** Genealogical questioning is separated from unsupported origin stories and inferred motives. User-directed, reversible practical revaluation is explicitly an adaptation, so no claim that Nietzsche endorsed these workplace procedures is made. The source link identifies the correct primary translated work: [The Genealogy of Morals](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52319/pg52319-images.html).
- **Popper:** The instructions avoid the serious caricatures of automatic disproof by one noisy observation, treating all untestable claims as meaningless, and crediting Popper with modern statistical tools. The reference explicitly labels its link as a secondary overview: [SEP, Karl Popper](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/popper/).
- **Rawls:** Priority of liberties, fair opportunity, the nontrivial informational role of the veil, and scope limitations are properly preserved. The reference correctly labels SEP as secondary; the one ambiguity above does not invalidate the remainder.
- **Socrates:** The text accurately presents examination of definitions as an adaptation of Plato's dialogues, explicitly disclaims access to the historical Socrates, and avoids concluding an opposite claim from a respondent's inability to answer. The linked primary translated dialogue is relevant: [Euthyphro](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/euthyfro.html).
- **Wittgenstein:** Later philosophy is explicitly distinguished from early work; examining uses, examples, and imported criteria is appropriate. The instructions preserve substantive disagreement and do not claim that operational specifications settle all philosophical questions. The source accurately identifies its linked overview as secondary: [SEP, Ludwig Wittgenstein](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein/).

## Verification limits

All eleven distinct cited source URLs were opened and matched the named works or scholarly overviews. Gutenberg catalogue metadata and access to its primary text pages were checked; this was not a line-by-line audit of every primary work. MIT primary translated texts and SEP overviews were consulted at relevant themes. No quotations are made in the skills, so edition-specific quotation accuracy was not at issue.

Popper's specified primary chapters, Rawls's published primary editions, and Wittgenstein's specified primary sections were not independently verified against full primary editions. SEP is authoritative secondary scholarship, not a substitute for those texts. Translation choices and contested interpretations remain limits, especially for Kant, Nietzsche, and Wittgenstein. A human scholar with relevant language and edition expertise should review any future historical claims or quotations. This review provides no evidence that the skills improve model performance; that requires separate evaluation.

The proposed Rawls correction was applied in version 0.1.1 after the two broad pilots. [Four focused forward checks](../evals/results/rawls-revision/REPORT.md) passed author inspection. The report above records the original independent review, including its limits.
